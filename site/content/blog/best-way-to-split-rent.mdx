Rent splitting is the #1 cause of roommate tension. When one person has the master bedroom with an en-suite bathroom and another is in a compact room sharing a hallway bath — paying equal rent doesn't feel fair.

Here's how to calculate rent fairly and keep the peace.

## The Room Size Method

The most common fair approach: split based on room size ratio.

**Formula**: (Your Room Sq Ft ÷ Total Bedroom Sq Ft) × Total Rent

### Example: 3-BHK Apartment, ₹45,000

| Room | Size | % of Rent | Monthly |
|------|------|-----------|---------|
| Master bedroom (en-suite) | 200 sq ft | 40% | ₹18,000 |
| Medium room | 160 sq ft | 32% | ₹14,400 |
| Small room | 140 sq ft | 28% | ₹12,600 |
| **Total bedrooms** | **500 sq ft** | **100%** | **₹45,000** |

## Adjusting for Amenities

Certain amenities increase a room's value:

- **Attached bathroom**: +5-10% of rent
- **Balcony access**: +3-5% of rent
- **Air conditioning**: +2-3% of rent
- **Better view/floor**: +2-5% of rent

## Utilities & Shared Expenses

Utilities should be split equally since everyone uses them:

- **Electricity**: Equal split (or by AC usage if tracked)
- **Water**: Equal split
- **Internet**: Equal split
- **Cleaning supplies**: Equal split
- **Cookware & kitchen**: Equal split

## Common Scenarios

### Couple in One Room
If a couple shares one room, they pay the room's share together. The room cost doesn't double — it stays the same.

### Occasional Guest
If someone's partner stays over 10+ nights a month, a small utility surcharge (~₹1,000-2,000) is reasonable.

### One Person Always Hosts
If someone's room is the hangout spot, consider a small discount (₹500-1,000) for the "common room" inconvenience.
