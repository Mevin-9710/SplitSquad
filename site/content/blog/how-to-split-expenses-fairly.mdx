Splitting expenses fairly is one of those things that sounds simple until you actually have to do it.

"Just divide by four" works great — until someone ordered the expensive wine, someone else skipped the appetizers, and another person paid for the last three rounds but nobody remembers who owes what anymore.

<HighlightBox variant="tip" title="The Golden Rule">
  The best split is the one everyone agrees on before the bill arrives. Communication beats calculation every time.
</HighlightBox>

## The Fair Split Framework

There's no single "fair" way to split expenses — it depends on the situation. Here are the most common approaches and when to use each.

### Equal Split

The simplest method. Divide the total by the number of people. Best for:

- Group meals where everyone ate similar items
- Trip expenses shared equally (rental cars, accommodation)
- Shared subscriptions and utilities
- Events and activities

### Custom Amounts

Each person pays exactly what they owe. Best for:

- Meals with clearly different orders
- When someone covers a large portion
- Splitting bills where consumption varies

### Percentage-Based

Split based on income, room size, or usage. Best for:

- Rent when rooms are different sizes
- Household bills based on occupancy
- Long-term shared expenses

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

<HighlightBox variant="warning" title="Watch Out">
  The biggest mistake groups make is waiting too long to settle up. The longer you wait, the more confusion builds. Settle within 24 hours whenever possible.
</HighlightBox>

- **Assuming everyone remembers**: They don't. Send payment requests immediately.
- **Mixing payment methods**: Stick to one (UPI is easiest for Indian groups).
- **Forgetting small expenses**: ₹50 chai runs add up over a month.
