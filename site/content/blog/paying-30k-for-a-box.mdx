You found a decent 3BHK in HSR Layout. Rent: ₹78,000. Three flatmates. The master bedroom has an attached bathroom and a balcony. The small room barely fits a bed and a study table.

Should everyone pay ₹26,000 each?

If you said yes, you've already had *that* conversation — the one where someone quietly resents paying the same for half the space.

## The Four Rent Split Methods

### 1. Equal Split — Simple but Unfair

Works when rooms are identical. Rare in real life.

### 2. Room-Size Weighted — The Gold Standard

The most recommended method. Each person pays proportional to their room size plus an equal share of common areas.

### 3. Amenity-Based — The Negotiator's Method

Some rooms have features worth paying extra for:

| Amenity | Typical Premium |
|---------|----------------|
| Attached bathroom | ₹1,500 - ₹3,000 |
| Balcony | ₹500 - ₹1,500 |
| AC pre-installed | ₹500 - ₹1,000 |
| Quieter side | ₹500 - ₹1,000 |

### 4. Income-Based — Tricky but Fair for Close Friends

When one person earns significantly more, some groups adjust. Use this carefully — it can create weird dynamics.

<HighlightBox variant="warning" title="The Couple Conundrum">
  In a 3BHK with a couple + one single, most Bangalore flats use 1.5x convention: the couple pays 1.5x what the single pays. Not 2x (unfair to the couple) and not equal (unfair to the single).
</HighlightBox>

## The Golden Rules of Flatmate Finance

1. **Settle weekly, not monthly.** Small frequent transfers feel neutral. ₹12,000 at month-end feels infuriating.

2. **Set a carry-forward threshold.** Agree that balances under ₹200 carry forward. Don't ask someone to UPI ₹47.

3. **Log expenses immediately.** Your roommate will not remember the ₹350 Swiggy order from Tuesday.

4. **Use a shared space for tracking.** A WhatsApp group with "you pay, I pay" gets messy fast. Use SplitSquad to keep everything in one place — rent, utilities, groceries, and one-tap UPI settlement.
